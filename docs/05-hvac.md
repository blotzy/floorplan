# HVAC and ventilation

[← Back to README](../README.md)

One fully ducted, variable-speed heat pump split into zones with motorized (electric) dampers, plus a continuously running exhaust fan for code-required fresh air. Every room gets ceiling registers. Capacities are rough; the HVAC contractor's Manual J sets the final sizes. No electric backup heat strips are needed in this climate.

| Zone | Serves | Thermostat |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Great room: kitchen, dining, lounge, foyer | Great room, on an interior wall away from the dining glass and the range |
| 2 | Gym, guest, office, guest ensuite, bedroom hall | Bedroom hall |
| 3 | Primary suite: bedroom, bath, dressing room | Primary bedroom |

Three zones are preferred. If the Manual J or the equipment's minimum airflow makes the primary suite too small to run as its own zone, merge zones 2 and 3 into one sleeping zone (two zones total), with balancing dampers on each bedroom branch.

**Heat pump and zoning**

- Outdoor unit (about 3 tons, variable-speed/inverter) on a noncombustible pad in the rear yard against the south wall behind the storage room (no windows on that stretch; short line set into the attic), with a dog guard. See [site](03-site-and-landscape.md).
- Matching variable-speed air handler in the sealed attic, near the attic hatch, over the primary dressing room (short line set to the rear condenser, condensate drain near the laundry's drains), on a service platform with a light and outlet. Secondary drain pan with a float switch under it; condensate drain route planned before drywall.
- Zoning from the equipment maker's own communicating zone panel (or one it approves), so the heat pump slows down to suit however many zones are calling. No bypass duct.
- Size each zone's ducts so the smallest zone alone can take the unit's minimum airflow without noise or short-cycling; the contractor confirms this with a Manual D duct design.
- Motorized dampers at the trunk takeoffs, reachable from the attic walkway. Manual balancing dampers on each branch.
- Central MERV 13 filter on the return at the air handler (media cabinet), for wildfire smoke; easy to reach for changes.
- Returns: a central return in the bedroom hall and one in the great room; transfer grilles or jumper ducts from each closed-door room (bedrooms, office, primary).
- Supplies: ceiling registers in the flat-ceiling rooms, near the windows and throwing across the room. The vaulted great room has no attic above it, so its supplies are high sidewall registers on the walls bordering the attic (the lounge/gym and kitchen/pantry walls), throwing across the room, with ducting in the insulated rafter bays only where unavoidable. The dining room's large south glass gets its own supply; the great room's return sits low on one of those walls. Destratification (a ceiling fan) helps under the high clerestory.
- Gym: keep registers out of the ring swing zone.
- Smoke alarms sit on the ceiling just inside each bedroom door (sheet A-2): keep supply registers at least 3' away and not blowing at them.
- Primary bedroom register aimed along the room, not at the bed.
- Primary, guest and office have ceiling fans: keep their registers out from under the blades and don't aim them at the fans.
- No return in the kitchen (grease) or the laundry (lint).
- Ducts stay in the sealed attic; no ducts, returns, or vent openings into the carport.
- Attic duct layout around the storage (decked areas on sheet A-7: the gym zone across the hall under the shed, and the strip along the gable's ridge over the hall): nothing crosses or runs under the decking. Two supply trunks along the ridge strip's long edges, one for the street-side rooms and one for the rear-side rooms, with each branch running outward through the low attic toward the street and rear walls, where the attic is too low for storage anyway. The great room's supplies run along the low rear edge of the shed attic, not across the gym storage. The hall return sits by the air handler so its duct stays short and out of the strip. Bath fan and fresh-air exhaust ducts also run outward to their terminations. Keep the walkway from the hatch to the air handler, and a path down the middle of the decking, clear. The Manual D duct design follows this layout.

**Ventilation**

- Whole-house ventilation: a quiet (1.0 sone or less), continuous-rated exhaust fan running all the time at the code's ventilation rate, in the common bath ceiling (central to the house), doubling as that bath's exhaust fan with a humidity boost, through its own ember-resistant roof cap with a damper. Pick one of the quietest continuous-rated models (about 0.3-0.5 sone), since the bath opens onto the bedroom hall. Outdoor air makes up through the envelope (exhaust-only); an ERV, if added, replaces it. The other bath fans run on demand (humidity or timer switches). No laundry fan or dryer vent (ventless heat pump dryer). Terminations: the range hood and the guest ensuite fan through the roof (the ensuite's on the wing's street slope), the common bath's (the continuous fan) through its own roof cap (separate from its skylight), and the primary bath's straight up from the bath through its own roof cap (a short vertical duct, staying in the low strip of attic by the rear wall). No wall caps.
- Range hood ducted outside through the roof, 400 CFM or less to avoid a makeup-air requirement.
- Network rack and water heater share the mechanical closet, which opens into the laundry through a louvered bifold so the water heater can draw on the laundry's air (and the rack's waste heat); no conditioned supply into the closet.
