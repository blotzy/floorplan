## Siting and carport

- House near the west side setback: **11'-6"** west side yard (11'-3⅝" required, 10% of the 113' lot width; confirm with the planner).
- **Carport 18' x 20'** on the east end, two cars side by side, **11'-6"** from the east property line. Clear-span beam with posts only at the corners (no middle post). The driveway is new: a single-car-width entrance between the parkway street tree and the tree just inside the lot, curving west to open onto the full carport width. The old driveway, further west in front of the planned porch and lounge, goes away. (No curbs or sidewalks on Laurel Drive, so there's no curb cut to deal with.) The street is a little higher than the carport, so the drive runs downhill toward the house: a trench drain or grading at the carport keeps runoff away from the house.
- Carport roof: nearly flat (about ¼" per foot), sloping east, away from the house, so water drains off its east edge into a gutter and downspouts tied to the site drainage, not toward the house or porch. Class A low-slope roofing (single-ply TPO/PVC or a mineral-surfaced modified-bitumen cap sheet over a noncombustible or fire-rated deck), with a thin dark fascia to match the house. Where it meets the house's east gable-end wall, flash it to the stucco.
- Carport: noncombustible framing and finishes; wall space for the EV charger. The exterior meter-main goes where SCE's service point is (the utilities are at the west end), so expect a long run from the panel to the carport charger, or a meter location on the east side if SCE allows.

## Drainage and root protection

- Site drains (area drains, the swale/gravel drain along the east wall, downspout lines) and the building sewer use smooth-wall, solid pipe with glued or gasketed root-resistant joints; no perforated pipe within root range of trees. Perforated drains, where needed, go in a filter-fabric wrapped gravel trench.
- Install vertical root barrier panels (about 2 ft deep, between trees and pipe runs) next to the street tree, the lot tree beside the driveway, and any tree planted near a drain or the sewer line. Plant trees at least 10 ft from drain and sewer lines.
- Cleanouts at each change of direction and at the ends of drain runs, so roots or debris can be cleared without digging; a backwater/cleanout at the sewer connection.

## Southeast courtyard (behind the carport, east of the house)

- **Dining patio** 12' x 21' along the house wall, from the carport back to the planters, outside the 8' dining slider. 6-seat table oriented north-south.
- **Pavers** 5'-deep band along the back of the carport (keeps the lawn out of the carport's 5' fire zone).
- **Lawn** about 15' x 14' (~210 sq ft), tall fescue + microclover blend, 1' DG edging. Irrigate from the unsoftened line.
- **Raised planters** 3' deep along the rear fence (noncombustible: concrete, block, or steel). Shade-tolerant, dog-safe plants (e.g., coral bells, ferns); no oleander, sago palm, foxglove, or azalea.
- **6' fence** across the back of the carport and bin strip with a **4' gate** behind the carport. The bin strip stays outside the dog yard.
- Optional: one small deciduous tree (e.g., crape myrtle) in the courtyard's southeast corner, ≥10' from the roof; nothing tall south of the house (future solar).

That stretch of wall has no windows; the nearest are the primary bath's tub window (~7' west) and the kitchen sink window (~9' east), with no bedrooms nearby. The line set runs straight up into the attic, toward the air handler near the hall.

## Front yard (no lawn)

- Native/Mediterranean low-water planting: dry creek bed / rain garden taking the downspouts, boulders, low shrubs screening the bedroom windows.
- **Entry court** in front of the porch, paved, edged on the street side by a seat-height masonry wall (noncombustible) that returns to the house just short of the lounge window. You walk in from the driveway side. Lit house numbers on the wall; low-voltage path lights.
- 5' gravel or paver strip along the house; no bark mulch within 30' of the house.
- Driveway: permeable pavers or concrete with gravel strips.
- **Privacy tree by the street, across from the lounge window:** an evergreen California native that grows fast, screens the big north window from the street, and has non-invasive roots. **Catalina cherry** (*Prunus ilicifolia* ssp. *lyonii*) is the first choice: evergreen, fast, dense, drought-tolerant, and its roots don't chase pipes. **Toyon** (*Heteromeles arbutifolia*) is a smaller, tough alternative; **Pacific wax myrtle** (*Morella californica*) works if it gets a little summer water. Avoid oaks and sycamores (big root zones), and resinous or oily plants such as pines, eucalyptus and juniper (fire risk).
  - Plant it inside the lot near the front line, not in the parkway, and slightly west of the window's centre: that keeps it at least 10 ft from the sewer line (it runs out to the cleanout near the porch) and from the driveway.
  - Fire-zone rules: nothing in the 5 ft noncombustible zone around the house; keep it limbed up, irrigated, and with its canopy clear of the roof and the eaves.
  - Put a root barrier between the tree and the sewer line (see drainage below).
