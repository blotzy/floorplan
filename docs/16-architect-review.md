## The project

- 220 Laurel Dr, Altadena (APN 5832-003-027), unincorporated LA County. Zone R-1, **Altadena Community Standards District (CSD)**.
- Eaton Fire rebuild, **non-like-for-like**: current zoning applies in full; 2022 California codes (Executive Order N-29-25, to confirm).
- **Very High Fire Hazard Severity Zone** (CRC R337 / Chapter 7A construction, Class A roof, fuel modification plan) and an **Alquist-Priolo Earthquake Fault Zone** (no liquefaction or landslide zone; whether a fault investigation or soils report is required is with the County).
- Owner-prepared drawings; a structural/civil engineer (engineer of record) does the structure, grading, drainage, erosion control, LID and a sump.
- Single story, all-electric, about 2,240 sq ft, 4 bedrooms (primary, gym, guest, office), 3 baths, attached open 2-car carport.

## Why the plan is the way it is

- **The site falls about 4' across the footprint**, east to west and front to back. The house steps with it as little as possible: one floor level, with the doors on the high sides.
- **Exterior doors only at grade.** The front door (on the street side) and the dining room's slider onto the courtyard (east, the high end of the lot) open at grade. **No doors on the rear (south) or west sides:** the ground falls away there, so a door would need a landing and steps, which the owner doesn't want (cost, and a raised landing or deck in the fire zone). The rear yard is reached around the house.
- **Bedrooms in a west wing, living at the east end:** the great room (foyer, lounge, dining, kitchen) sits by the carport and entry; the bedrooms are down a hall at the quiet west end, with the laundry, pantry and mechanical block between them as a sound buffer and one plumbing core.
- **Split roof (shed + gable):** the shed over the great room rises to the street to fit a clerestory band over the lounge and gives the great room its sloped ceiling; the gable keeps the bedroom wing low. Both slope to the rear (SSW), one plane, for solar (about 10 kW planned, room for much more).
- **Carport and porch** are shaped by the County's parking rule (parking behind the front door): the qualifying covered porch lets the carport start at the house front, with its front 4' a covered walkway.
- **Street windows recessed and the clerestory flush:** articulation for the County standard, without a high ledge that would catch embers.
- **Fire-zone choices throughout:** stucco, Class A shingles, sealed attic, few roof penetrations, no dryer vent, gravel and pavers in the ember zone.

## Not settled: please weigh in

1. **Primary bath shower storage.** The shower uses a tiled ledge wall for bottles. Not sure it's the best answer: alternatives could be a recessed niche in an interior wall, a shelf, or a different layout of the wet room.
2. **Primary bedroom doors.** The bedroom has a lot of doors and openings (hall door, walk-in closet, bath, the reading nook). A version that entered through the walk-in closet didn't feel right either. Is there a cleaner arrangement?
3. **Storage.** Less storage than wanted overall: closets, linen, garage-type storage (there's no garage, only an open carport), and bulk storage. Where could more go without growing the footprint (lot coverage has about 160 sq ft to spare)?
4. **Dining room size.** The dining area feels like a lot of floor dedicated to one use. Could it shrink, or double as something else, to give space to storage or the bedrooms?
5. **Anything else** the architect sees in circulation, proportions, or how the rooms work together.
