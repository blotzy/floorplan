# Plumbing

[← Back to README](../README.md)

The water service enters at the northwest corner (the utilities are at the west end). Unsoftened lines branch off first, then a single main runs to the mechanical closet, where everything downstream is softened.

Interior supply piping is copper. No PEX except for the fire sprinklers (a standalone NFPA 13D system; sprinklers are required for all new single-family homes, including fire rebuilds).

**Existing connections (survey):** the water meter is in the parkway at the west end of the frontage; the sewer cleanout is at the front of the lot, roughly in line with the porch and lounge, so the building sewer runs out under the front of the house to it. The lot falls toward the west and rear, so check the sewer slope from the far (west) baths to that cleanout early. Use solid, root-resistant pipe and joints for the building sewer, with root barriers wherever it passes within ~10 ft of a tree (the street-side privacy tree and the existing trees near the front); see [site drainage](03-site-and-landscape.md#drainage-and-root-protection).

**Water service order:** meter → main shutoff and pressure regulator → smart leak-detection shutoff valve → unsoftened branches → main line to the mechanical closet → sediment pre-filter → softener → water heater, manifold, and the RO filter (drinking water to the kitchen sink tap and the fridge).

**Irrigation rough-in** (fuel modification plan FM-1 and the state's water-efficient landscape rules)

- Unsoftened irrigation branch, backflow preventer and valve manifold as above; drip zones for trees, shrubs and ground cover, a spray zone only for the courtyard lawn, no overspray onto paving.
- Weather- or soil-moisture-based controller (see the electrical doc for its outlet).
- Sleeves (empty 2"–3" pipes) under the driveway, carport slab, entry court and courtyard pavers before they're poured or laid, so irrigation lines (and future low-voltage landscape lighting) can cross without cutting paving.

**Bathrooms**

- Use epoxy or pre-mixed urethane grout for shower tile work.

**Kitchen**

- Integrated drain board on the dishwasher side of the sink (countertop with a slight slope and grooves).
- Coffee station on the peninsula counter over the drink fridge.
- Prep sink on the peninsula, about 10' out from the rear wall: its drain runs in the slab and it can't vent through a wall, so it gets island fixture venting (California's plumbing code generally doesn't allow air-admittance valves): a vent loop rising as high as possible inside the sink cabinet, back down through the floor, a foot vent tying it to the drain below the slab, then a horizontal run under the slab to a riser in the wall. The two sinks share one vent: the prep sink's under-slab run goes along the peninsula to the rear wall, and its riser joins the main sink's vent there at least 6" above the main sink's flood rim (per code); each sink keeps its own trap. No extra roof penetration. A cleanout on the loop, reachable inside the sink cabinet. Lay it all out on the plumbing plan before the slab is poured. Softened hot and cold.
- **Plumbing vents, kept few and simple:** the kitchen sink's vent rises in the rear wall beside its window and goes straight up through the roof at the wall line, near the rear eave, below the first solar array (the prep sink's riser joins it). The laundry and the common and primary baths vent up through the attic, combined where it's a short run, through one or two vents on the wing's rear slope. The guest ensuite has its own vent on the wing's street slope. About three or four roof vents in all, each outside the first solar array (see the [solar layout](../renders/experiments/solar-layout.png)), at least 6" above the roof and 10' from (or 3' above) openable windows and air intakes. No dryer vent (ventless heat pump dryer).
- **Fire zone:** plumbing vents are open pipes, not the ventilation openings the wildfire rules regulate, so they need no screens; use metal flashings rather than rubber boots. Every exhaust termination (bath fans, the whole-house fan, the range hood) is metal with a backdraft damper, ember-resistant where a listed product exists; the attic is sealed, so there are no attic, soffit or ridge vents.

**Equipment and fixtures**

- Whole-house water softener with sediment pre-filter, in the mechanical closet. The softener needs a drain with an air gap.
- Heat pump water heater, 80 gal, in the mechanical closet, with a condensate drain and a drain pan with a leak sensor; 6-12" of service clearance above it.
- Thermostatic mixing valve at the water heater outlet: the tank stores at about 140°F (more usable hot water for the double shower and back-to-back showers, more solar energy stored, and Legionella control) and the valve sends tempered water (about 120°F) to the house. Shower and tub valves keep their own anti-scald protection.
- Tankless reverse-osmosis filter in the mechanical closet, fed from the softened line (softening first extends the membrane's life). Food-grade lines run to the RO tap at the kitchen sink and to the fridge, with accessible shutoffs at both ends. Its waste line drains through an air gap or standpipe; it needs an outlet, and shares the closet's leak pan/sensor. A bypass plug replaces the fridge's internal filter. The sink cabinet stays free for the trash pull-out.
- Leak pans under the washer and fridge.
- Primary shower (double, curbless): two wall heads side by side on the rear wall, each on its own thermostatic or pressure-balance valve, so each valve serves one outlet and stays within California's 1.8 gpm limit for multiple shower outlets (CALGreen 4.303.1.3.2); confirm the approach with plan check. Both valves on the rear wall by the walk-in entry, reachable before stepping into the spray. The rear wall is an exterior wall: the supplies drop from the sealed (conditioned) attic in the stud bays either side of the shower's window (the window is centred between the heads to keep those bays clear), with the pipes on the warm side of the cavity insulation. Linear drain along the glass line; one waterproofed zone (membrane floor, walls and the solid bench), slab recessed for the slope. No ceiling rain head.
- Bidet seats at all three baths. Plan toilet locations so each bidet outlet has enough separation from tubs and showers.
- Small utility sink beside the washer on the laundry's bath wall. The heat pump dryer's condensate drains into the washer standpipe.

**Optional**

- No hot water recirculation. Optional: while the walls are open, run a return line from the primary bath (the farthest fixtures) back to the water heater, capped, so an on-demand recirculation pump can be added later without opening walls.
