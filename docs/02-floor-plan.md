# Architect: floor plan, roof, finishes

[← Back to README](../README.md)

These are the rooms, spaces, and openings the plan must provide so the trades can do their work. The plan drawing and the schematic show one way to fit them; sizes and fine details are the architect's call.

## Entry, foyer, and lounge

- **Corner porch** at the lounge/carport (northeast) corner, set into the house, with a post at the house's corner where the carport beam lands. It qualifies as a covered entry porch for the County's parking rules (Table 22.140.520-D: at least 8' wide and 5' deep, its ceiling no more than 12' above its floor): its floor runs a little past the house front toward the street, under a slim flat metal canopy at the porch ceiling's height, and a little into the carport's covered walkway, while the front door stays at the back of the notch. Front step in front. A wood-look feature wall (noncombustible: fiber-cement panels or porcelain wood-grain tile, in vertical boards) on the porch's back walls, around the front door and the reeded window, wrapping onto the porch ceiling.
- **Front door** on the street-facing side of the porch, hinged on the east side so it opens flat against the foyer's east wall. No sidelight, so the door itself has glass: one horizontal rectangular lite near the top of the door (tempered, dual-pane, reeded or frosted, meeting the wildfire rules for exterior doors), lighting the foyer and echoing the small high bathroom window on the same street front.
- **Reeded-glass window** in the porch's side wall, so you can see who's at the door from the lounge. Narrow reeds or satin-reeded, dual-pane, low-E, tempered, NFRC-rated, from the same window line as the rest of the house.
- **Foyer:** the **foyer wall** (stopping short of the lobby) separates foyer and lounge; its lounge side is the **TV wall**, and its foyer side is one built-in: a closet at each end with a bench niche between them (coat hooks above, baskets below), across from the front door's swing. Shoes and coats stay behind closed doors, so nothing is on show when the door opens; the closets can continue up toward the sloped ceiling for seasonal storage. The closet at the dining-room end, directly behind the TV, doubles as the **AV closet** (receiver and other set-and-forget gear on a vented shelf, with a conduit through the wall to the TV). See [inspiration](15-inspiration.md#foyer-storage).
- **Lounge:** large north window centred on the wall. One L sectional, its window leg floated off the window and its other leg facing the TV wall. Square coffee table. All furniture stays clear of the walkway from the foyer to the bedroom hall.
- **Hearth bench:** a built-in bench on the TV wall, centred on the TV: from just past the reeded window to just short of the walkway, at seat height and deep enough for a receiver in a cubby. Framed and drywalled on a recessed base so it reads as floating. A linear electric fireplace set high in its face, centred under the TV, which hangs just above the bench; two matching open cubbies, one each side of the fireplace, for streamers and consoles (line of sight). The street-wall corner by the reeded window stays open floor (a reading chair). Blocking in the wall for the TV mount. The fireplace is installed to its listing: choose a model rated for a bench or shelf directly above it, venting heat to the front or bottom, and build the bench to its installation sheet.

## Dining

- **Dining:** table centred on the south window.
- **Sliding door** on the east wall, just past the courtyard fence, leaving solid wall at the dining room's southeast corner (where the dining window turns it), opening onto an at-grade landing in the gravel strip along the house (no patio or deck).
- **South window:** one wide window across most of the dining wall (two mulled units), with solid wall left at each end, centred on the wall, with a low sill (seated-height view of the garden) and its head in line with the other windows, including the kitchen sink window beside it, which sits higher to clear the counter. It faces SSW (azimuth ~201°), so it takes strong low afternoon sun that the eaves won't block: plan an exterior shade or awning and low-SHGC glass, and check it against the Title 24 glazing budget. If the window's size leaves too little wall at the southeast corner (with the slider around that corner) and the engineer needs steel framing or a steel hold-down there, reconsider: a narrower window that leaves more wall at each end is the fallback.

## Kitchen

See [kitchen](08-kitchen.md). A comfortable work aisle, a 30" range, a 36" fridge, a single deep sink under the window, and a peninsula with three stools (with knee overhang) facing the dining area.

## Pantry, laundry, mechanical, common bath (the block behind the kitchen)

Three strips side by side, each with its own door off the main lobby (no side hall):

- **Pantry:** door at the corner by the fridge, hinged so it opens flat against the kitchen wall. Shelves on three sides (the shelves by the door start past the door leaf). Outlets inside.
- **Mechanical / network closet:** behind the pantry at the south end of the strip, opening into the laundry through a **louvered** bifold (the heat pump water heater needs the laundry's air volume). Holds the heat pump water heater, softener and brine tank, sediment pre-filter, tankless RO filter, water distribution manifold, and the network rack (kept clear of water lines). Floor drain or drain pan. No conditioned supply into the closet.
- **Laundry:** a galley running the full depth of the block, with its own door off the lobby. Everything is on the wall shared with the common bath (the plumbing wall): a folding counter right inside the door with shelves above it and the utility sink set into its far end (the robot vacuum dock in an open, floor-level bay in the counter base beside the sink cabinet, sharing the sink's cold supply and trapped drain), then the front-loading dryer and washer side by side on pedestals (no bending in the narrow aisle). The dryer sits next to the folding counter and the washer beyond it, so with the washer on the left as you face them, the doors open away from each other (most front-load washer doors hinge on the left and can't be reversed; confirm with the chosen models). The washer box (supplies and drain) goes behind the washer. The opposite (pantry-side) wall stays open floor, apart from the panel near the door. The mech closet's bifold and the storage room open off the back of the galley, on opposite side walls; open shelves on the rear wall between them, kept short of both openings and stopping below a high window (sill above head height, like the primary bath's) that brings in daylight and opens for fresh air. Heat pump (ventless) dryer, so no dryer duct to the outside. Leak pan under the washer. No exhaust fan (and no wall cap): the ventless dryer adds little moisture, the heat pump water heater next door dries the laundry's air as it runs, and the high window opens for fresh air. Solid-core door and insulated walls to keep machine noise out of the bedroom hall.
- **Indoor electrical subpanel** flush in the laundry's pantry-side wall just past the swing of the laundry door (never behind the open door), with its code working space kept clear across the open floor and up to the required height; nothing else on that wall.
- **Common bath:** own door off the lobby, at the bath's west end (so the toilet is out of view from the lounge). A narrow three-quarter bath with its sink, toilet and shower drain on the wall shared with the laundry, so one plumbing wall serves both rooms: sink by the door, then the toilet, then a curbless shower across the far end with a linear drain and a fixed glass panel, entered from the door side. The shower's valve and head are on its back wall (shared with the storage room) at the entry end: the valve beside the entry, so the water can be started before stepping into the spray, and the head just past it on an adjustable arm angled toward the laundry-wall end, away from the opening; their supply drops from the attic, with an access panel for the valve on the storage-room side. The end wall shared with the primary suite carries no plumbing. No window (exhaust fan). Solid-core door. A tubular skylight (about 10") over the floor between the sink and the shower brings in daylight; use a combined skylight and exhaust-fan unit so it's one roof opening, with a dome and flashing rated for the fire zone. The plumbing vent for the rear fixtures comes through the roof just downslope of the dome (in line with it, within one panel position), so the solar layout works around one cluster.
- **Storage room** behind the common bath, open to the back of the laundry through a cased opening (no door). Shelves on the primary-bath side. Possible chest freezer (a small, roughly 7 cu ft model fits) on the floor against the rear wall, beside the shelves, with its own outlet; keep the cased opening clear so it can be carried in.

## Bedroom wing

- **Gym:** North window matching the office's. Mineral wool in the walls shared with the lounge end. Outlet for a stationary bike.
- **Gym ring mounts:** two ceiling points near the room's centre (the 9' ceiling suits strength and static ring work; kipping and muscle-ups are tight at that height), swinging along the room's long direction. The truss designer must carry a concentrated load of about 500 lb at each point (blocking across several trusses with framing connectors; through-bolted forged eye bolts with plate washers). Keep lights, fans, HVAC registers, and the smoke alarm out of the swing zone. Photograph the blocking before drywall.
- **Guest:** door at the bath end of the hall wall, with one long closet on the rest of that wall. A hinged door into the guest ensuite, in the wall between them just inside the entry and swinging into the guest room, is the bath's only door: it is the guest room's private bath.
- **Office:** a high window on the west wall, above the bed or desk wall, for cross-breeze with the street window and privacy from the neighbour's two-story house close to the west line. Solid-core door and insulated walls for calls; wall blocking for monitor arms and shelves; dimmable ceiling light.
- **Guest ensuite:** reached only from the guest room. A standard alcove tub with a shower head and curtain. Its street window is a small high one over the tub (sill above the shower head, head in line with the other windows; tempered, obscure glass, fiberglass or vinyl frame, tile sill sloped toward the tub), so it stays out of the spray. Pick the tub model before framing so the alcove is framed to its spec. The tub's drain, valve and shower head, the WC and the vanity all share the east wall (the plumbing wall with the gym). A wall-to-wall linen closet along the hall wall, opening into the bath (bifold doors), fills the space where a hall door would otherwise be. Everyone else uses the common bath off the lobby.
- **Primary bath:** no tub (the guest ensuite has the house's tub). A curbless double shower fills the end by the common bath (a plain 2x4 wall with sound insulation; nothing is plumbed in it), closed by a fixed glass panel from the closet wall, with a walk-in entry beside the rear wall. Two shower heads on the rear wall, a station each, spray toward the closet wall; each has its own valve, and both valves sit by the entry, so the water can be started without stepping into the spray. Their supplies drop from the sealed attic in the rear wall's stud bays either side of the shower's window, on the warm side of the insulation. A solid built-in bench (foam bench kit or block, wrapped in the shower membrane) runs the full width of the shower along the closet wall, facing the heads. A recessed niche in the rear wall, centred between the heads below the window and matching its width, within reach of both stations and out of the direct spray (rigid foam insulation behind it, since it's an exterior wall). Linear drain along the glass line. The WC and a double vanity sit on the closet (north) wall beside the shower, so that wall carries their supplies, drains and vent; the plumber sets its framing. The rear wall stays clear as the walkway from the door to the shower entry. Two high rear windows, heads in line with the other windows: a short strip in the shower, centred between the heads with its sill above them (wet zone: tempered, sloped tile sill), and a larger one over the walkway with its sill above eye level. Privacy without obscure glass and light across the ceiling. The door is from the primary bedroom, at the rear-wall end of the bath, and opens flat against the rear wall. See [plumbing](04-plumbing.md).
- **Walk-in closet:** between the primary bedroom and the laundry block, behind the bath, entered from the bedroom through a hinged door that swings into the closet and opens flat against the hall-side wall. Deep enough for hanging rods on both long sides with a comfortable aisle between them: the one on the bath side runs to the bedroom wall; the one on the entry side stops short of it to leave room for the door and a landing inside it. No door to the bath. The far end wall is one built-in: corner shelves (shoes, folded clothes, bags) either side of a drawer stack.
- **Primary suite:** the bedroom door is on the south side of the hall at the closet corner, a few steps from the walk-in closet and bath doors, hinged on its far side so the open door never blocks the closet door. Beyond the hall's end wall, the hall's last stretch is the bedroom's **reading nook**, open to the room along its street side: a built-in bench the length of the office wall and a window in the nook's west end above the bench (afternoon and indirect light; low-solar-gain glass and a shade for summer afternoons). The nook's ceiling drops below the bedroom's, which makes it cozier, with reading lights recessed in it; above the lowered ceiling, built-in cabinets run the nook's length with their doors facing the bedroom (seasonal and bulky storage, reached with a step stool). The nook window's head sits a little below the lowered ceiling, with a band of wall above it. A modest south window, since it faces SSW into a sleeping room; shade it with the overhang.

- **Interior doors:** solid core throughout, for sound and so the tall doors stay flat (the mechanical closet keeps its louvered bifold).
- **Lobby:** the bedroom hall runs from the lounge to a plain end wall, with the office and primary doors facing each other just before it; no exterior door at the west end. Partway down, just past the common bath, a cased opening (no door) under a dropped header, lower than the ceiling, marks the start of the bedroom end and breaks up the length of the hall. A tubular skylight in the hall ceiling, just short of the end wall, daylights the end of the hall (larger than the common bath's; fire-zone-rated dome and flashing, insulated tube through the attic). It's centred in the hall and takes the last recessed-light spot on its centreline; in the attic the tube jogs toward the street (two adjustable elbows) to a dome on the gable's street slope just below the ridge, off the solar side.

## Ceilings and roof

- **Vaulted great room:** the foyer, lounge, dining room and kitchen share one continuous sloped ceiling following the roof, highest at the clerestory and lowest at the rear wall, with no step between rooms. The foyer/TV wall runs full height up to the sloped ceiling, so the foyer is a tall space of its own, open toward the dining room. Insulation goes at the roof deck (spray foam in the rafter bays). Light fixtures, fans, smoke alarms and sprinkler heads in the vault are laid out and wired before insulation, with a few spare conduits up the rafter bays; prefer surface, pendant or slim fixtures over recessed cans. The range hood ducts straight up through the roof.
- Everywhere else: flat 9' ceilings under the sealed attic. The ducted heat pump unit and its ducts go in that attic (over the bedroom wing and utility block), so no hallway drop ceiling is needed.
- The clerestory glass counts toward the Title 24 glazing budget, which keeps the house just under the 20% prescriptive limit: confirm with the energy consultant.
- Ceiling box or low-voltage bracket at the two Wi-Fi access point locations, installed at rough-in with the Cat6A ending inside.
- Ceiling fans: short downrods under the 9' ceilings, longer ones (or sloped-ceiling mounts) in the vault.
- The shed and the gable's rear slope face plan-south (true azimuth ~201°, SSW, fine for solar); the gable's street slope is not for solar, so the guest ensuite's plumbing vent and the hall skylight's dome go there. On the solar side, keep roof penetrations out of the first array (on the shed, east of the range hood cap) and the room beside it to grow: the range hood cap, the kitchen sink's vent at the rear wall line, the common bath's skylight and one or two plumbing vents on the wing's rear slope. No tall trees south of the house.
- Solar mounting (flashed attachments and rails) installed with the roofing over the first array on the shed; panels added later. Conduit from the array into the attic and to the panel, plus a conduit run to a future battery location.
- **Roof form:** a midcentury-modern **split roof**. A **shed** runs from the porch to the gym/ensuite wall, rising from the rear wall to the street, and covers the whole great room and the gym; its tall street wall carries the **clerestory band** over the lounge. The rest of the bedroom wing is under a low **gable** with its ridge down the middle of the house; its rear slope lines up with the shed's, so the rear of the house reads as one roof plane, and at the street the shed stands above the wing. The shed's side wall where it rises above the gable gets step and kick-out flashing. Eaves as deep as the yard rules allow (they may project only so far into a required yard, §22.110.090.A, and the front, rear and west walls sit on their setback lines) and a thin dark fascia; a gutter along the rear; the wing's street eave has none (no gutter on the street side to catch embers and leaves), dripping to a gravel trench in the noncombustible strip. No butterfly or near-flat roofs over the house. If the budget needs it, the fallback is an MCM low gable with flat ceilings throughout (see the 3D model's Roof menu).
- **Asphalt shingle roof** over the sealed attic (unvented deck allowed by CRC R806.5): dark Class A fiberglass shingles, wind-rated for high-wind areas (ASTM D7158 Class H or D3161 Class F) and installed with the maker's high-wind nailing and sealed starter strips at the eaves and rakes. The roof is at the shingles' minimum slope, so it needs two layers of underlayment over the whole deck (CRC R905.2), with self-adhered underlayment at the eaves and along the shed / gable junction. Eave and rake edges and the ridge cap closed so embers can't get under the shingles. Get the shingle maker's warranty for an unvented (spray-foam) deck and the roofer's workmanship warranty in writing. Future solar mounts on rails over flashed attachments into the framing.
- **Street facade articulation** (County single-family design standards, §22.140.520; see the [zoning check](11-zoning-check.md)): the punched street windows (lounge, gym, guest, office and the guest ensuite's high window) are set deep in the wall, recessed behind the stucco face, over half the street facade's window area; the clerestory band above stays flush, a crisp ribbon of glass under the roof. At the rear, thin flat metal canopies shade the kitchen and primary bedroom windows; each runs only a little past its window and has no posts. Canopies noncombustible.
- **Exterior walls: stucco** (noncombustible), in a light MCM palette with a dark fascia and a bright accent front door.

## Sealed attic and storage

- Insulation at the roofline: spray foam under the roof deck or rigid board above it. No attic vents.
- Attic storage loading (about 20 psf) over the gym, where the shed roof gives the attic its height, specified to the truss designer, with plywood decking over it. Under the wing's gable the attic is tallest along the ridge over the hall and tapers to very little toward the street and rear walls. Keep it clear of the equipment platform and walkway by the hatch.
- Insulated, gasketed attic access panel (no pull-down ladder; a portable ladder reaches it) in the bedroom hall ceiling across from the walk-in closet wall, large enough to pass the air handler out for service or replacement. Keep the hall's Wi-Fi access point, return grille and smoke alarm clear of it. Light switch at the hatch and an outlet in the attic. Code wants at least 30" of headroom above the opening and a 30"-high, floored passage to the air handler within 20' (CRC R807.1, M1305.1.3); the builder's target is 36" at the opening, which the gable gives over the hall, under its ridge. Put the air handler beside the hatch, near the ridge, where the wing's attic is tallest.
- Equipment access per mechanical code (opening, walkway, and service platform with a light and outlet) at the ducted unit. Place that equipment near the hatch.
- Thermal barrier over the spray foam (drywall or an approved coating), since the attic will be used for storage.
- Smurf tube or a cable tray across the attic from the network rack for future runs.
- Keep heavy items and heat-producing equipment such as servers out of the attic.

## Wildfire hardening

- Enclosed eaves with noncombustible or ignition-resistant soffits (wood-look is fine if it's fire-retardant-treated or metal/fiber cement).
- Every remaining opening fire-resistant: exhaust terminations (bath fans, the whole-house fan, the range hood) in metal with backdraft dampers, ember-resistant where a listed product exists; no attic, soffit or ridge vents (sealed attic); Class A skylights; plumbing vents with metal flashings; no dryer vent (ventless dryer); foundation vents only if the floor is raised, and then ember-resistant.
- Stucco walls (noncombustible) and tempered dual-pane windows.
- A noncombustible zone around the house (pavers/gravel, no mulch or turf), at least as wide as code requires.
- Sealed (unvented) attic with no attic vents, so embers have no entry point.
- Metal (or other noncombustible) fencing where any fence meets the house, for the code-required distance.
