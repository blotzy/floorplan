# Architect: floor plan, roof, finishes

[← Back to README](../README.md)

These are the rooms, spaces, and openings the plan must provide so the trades can do their work. The plan drawing and the schematic show one way to fit them; sizes and fine details are the architect's call.

## Entry, foyer, and lounge

- **Corner porch** at the lounge/carport (northeast) corner, set into the house, with a post at the outside corner where the carport beam lands. Front step in front. A wood-look feature wall (noncombustible: fiber-cement panels or porcelain wood-grain tile, in vertical boards) on the porch's back walls, around the front door and the reeded window, wrapping onto the porch ceiling.
- **Front door** on the street-facing side of the porch, hinged on the east side so it opens flat against the foyer's east wall. No sidelight, so the door itself has glass: one horizontal rectangular lite near the top of the door (tempered, dual-pane, reeded or frosted, meeting the wildfire rules for exterior doors), lighting the foyer and echoing the small high bathroom window on the same street front.
- **Reeded-glass window** in the porch's side wall, so you can see who's at the door from the lounge. Narrow reeds or satin-reeded, dual-pane, low-E, tempered, NFRC-rated, from the same window line as the rest of the house.
- **Foyer:** the **foyer wall** (stopping short of the lobby) separates foyer and lounge; its lounge side is the **TV wall**, and its foyer side holds a closet and bench, across from the front door's swing.
- **Lounge:** large north window centred on the wall. One L sectional, its window leg floated off the window and its other leg facing the TV wall,. Square coffee table. All furniture stays clear of the walkway from the foyer to the bedroom hall.

## Dining

- **Dining:** table centred on the south window.
- **Sliding door** on the east wall, centred between the carport fence and the house's southeast corner, opening onto the dining patio.
- **South window:** one wide window across most of the dining wall (two mulled units), with solid wall left at each end, centred on the wall, with a low sill (seated-height view of the garden) and its head in line with the other windows, including the kitchen sink window beside it, which sits higher to clear the counter. It faces SSW (azimuth ~201°), so it takes strong low afternoon sun that the eaves won't block: plan an exterior shade or awning and low-SHGC glass, and check it against the Title 24 glazing budget. If the window's size leaves too little wall at the southeast corner (with the slider around that corner) and the engineer needs steel framing or a steel hold-down there, reconsider: a narrower window that leaves more wall at each end is the fallback.

## Kitchen

See [kitchen](08-kitchen.md). A comfortable work aisle, a 30" range, a 36" fridge, a single deep sink under the window, and a peninsula with three stools (with knee overhang) facing the dining area.

## Pantry, laundry, mechanical, common bath (the block behind the kitchen)

Three strips side by side, each with its own door off the main lobby (no side hall):

- **Pantry:** door at the corner by the fridge, hinged so it opens flat against the kitchen wall. Shelves on three sides (the shelves by the door start past the door leaf). Outlets inside.
- **Mechanical / network closet:** behind the pantry at the south end of the strip, opening into the laundry through a **louvered** bifold (the heat pump water heater needs the laundry's air volume). Holds the heat pump water heater, softener and brine tank, sediment pre-filter, tankless RO filter, water distribution manifold, and the network rack (kept clear of water lines). Floor drain or drain pan. No conditioned supply into the closet.
- **Laundry:** a galley running the full depth of the block, with its own door off the lobby. Everything is on the wall shared with the common bath (the plumbing wall): a folding counter right inside the door with shelves above it and the utility sink set into its far end, then the front-loading dryer and washer side by side on pedestals (no bending in the narrow aisle). The dryer sits next to the folding counter and the washer beyond it, so with the washer on the left as you face them, the doors open away from each other (most front-load washer doors hinge on the left and can't be reversed; confirm with the chosen models). The washer box (supplies and drain) goes behind the washer. The opposite (pantry-side) wall stays open floor, apart from the panel near the door. A pulley-operated ceiling airer (drying rack) over the aisle uses the ceiling height for hang-drying; it needs blocking in the ceiling for its pulleys and a wall cleat. The mech closet's bifold and the storage room open off the back of the galley, on opposite side walls; open shelves on the rear wall between them, kept short of both openings and stopping below a high window (sill above head height, like the primary bath's) that brings in daylight and opens for fresh air. Heat pump (ventless) dryer, so no dryer duct to the outside. Leak pan under the washer. Exhaust fan as well. Solid-core door and insulated walls to keep machine noise out of the bedroom hall.
- **Indoor electrical subpanel** flush in the laundry's pantry-side wall near the door, with its code working space kept clear across the open floor; nothing else on that wall.
- **Common bath:** own door off the lobby, beside the laundry door. A narrow three-quarter bath with every fixture on the wall shared with the primary bath: sink by the door, then the toilet, then a curbless shower across the far end with a linear drain and a fixed glass panel, entered from the door side. No window (exhaust fan). Solid-core door. A tubular skylight (about 10") over the floor between the sink and the shower brings in daylight; use a combined skylight and exhaust-fan unit so it's one roof opening, with a dome and flashing rated for the fire zone, placed with the solar layout.
- **Storage room** behind the common bath, open to the back of the laundry through a cased opening (no door). Shelves on the primary-bath side.
- **Robot vacuum dock** niche at the bottom of the storage room's shelves, open to the floor; it needs water and a trapped drain (see plumbing).

## Bedroom wing

- **Gym:** North window matching the office's. Mineral wool in the walls shared with the lounge end. ERV boost control. Outlet for a stationary bike.
- **Gym ring mounts:** two ceiling points near the room's centre (the 9' ceiling suits strength and static ring work; kipping and muscle-ups are tight at that height), swinging along the room's long direction. The truss designer must carry a concentrated load of about 500 lb at each point (blocking across several trusses with framing connectors; through-bolted forged eye bolts with plate washers). Keep lights, fans, HVAC/ERV registers, and the smoke alarm out of the swing zone. Photograph the blocking before drywall.
- **Guest:** door at the bath end of the hall wall, with one long closet on the rest of that wall. A hinged door into the shared ensuite, in the wall between them just inside the entry and swinging into the guest room, makes it the guest's private bath when someone is staying.
- **Office:** Solid-core door and insulated walls for calls; wall blocking for monitor arms and shelves; dimmable ceiling light.
- **Shared ensuite:** a standard alcove tub with a shower head and curtain. Its street window is a small high one over the tub (sill above the shower head, head in line with the other windows; tempered, obscure glass, fiberglass or vinyl frame, tile sill sloped toward the tub), so it stays out of the spray. Pick the tub model before framing so the alcove is framed to its spec. The tub's drain, valve and shower head, the WC and the vanity all share the east wall (the plumbing wall with the gym). Linen closet in the corner by the hall door. Two doors: the hall door and one from the guest room, both with privacy locks, so the hall door can be locked while guests stay (everyone else uses the common bath).
- **Primary bath wet room:** a back-to-wall freestanding soaking tub (flat back and squared ends, so it sits tight to the plumbing wall with only small gaps at its ends; freestanding look from the front, nothing to clean behind it), filled from a wall-mounted filler on the plumbing wall. **Tub selection:** at least 6" clear between the tub and the end walls at the floor, so the ends can be wiped and rinsed; a tub with sloped or flared sides can have a longer rim than base (check the spec sheet's base length, not just the overall length), as long as the rim still clears the walls by about 2". Plus a wide curbless shower beside it with two shower heads facing each other (closet wall and rear wall), with a linear drain, closed in by glass running wall to wall along its open side: a fixed panel from the closet wall and a hinged glass door at the rear-wall end, swinging out into the bath. The door hangs directly off the end of the ledge wall (glass mounted onto the ledge, no gap or inside corner that's hard to clean). Each head has its own independent control (two people can shower at once, each setting their own temperature), and a handheld on a slide bar sits on the rear (ledge) side beside the everyday head, sharing that head's valve through a diverter. The rear-wall head, by the entry, is the everyday one. Bottle storage is a ledge wall under it: the shower's rear wall is built out below chest height into the shower, topped with a tiled shelf (sloped to drain) across the full width of the shower, with that head's valve and supply inside the thickened part (off the exterior wall). Its valve is reachable from the entry before stepping into the spray. Keep the door opening its full width past the ledge. The WC and a double vanity sit on the closet (north) wall beside the shower, so that wall carries their supplies, drains and vent; the plumber sets its framing. The rear wall stays clear as the walkway from the door to the shower entry. Two high horizontal rear windows (standard awning or fixed sizes, the one over the tub a little narrower to stay clear of the rear shower head), sills above eye level and heads in line with the other windows: one over the tub (tempered, in the wet zone), and one over the walkway. Privacy without obscure glass, light across the ceiling, and no wet sill at the tub. The door is from the primary bedroom, at the rear-wall end of the bath, and opens flat against the rear wall. See [plumbing](04-plumbing.md).
- **Primary bath look (reference):** [this photo](https://preview.redd.it/y02s1n6m16rf1.jpeg?width=3024&format=pjpg&auto=webp&s=c40dec3d4f6da52e96ee1c2b1c0515655f252d6d) is the model: one curbless wet room holding both the freestanding tub and the shower, tiled floor to ceiling in a light vertical tile, a patterned feature-tile panel on the wall behind the tub, small penny-round mosaic on the sloped wet-room floor, larger dark tiles outside it, a recessed niche, a handheld on a slide bar plus a ceiling rain head, and a teak bench. Differences from the photo: here the tub has a flat back against the plumbing wall, bottles go on the ledge wall, and glass with a door closes the wet room's open side.
- **Walk-in closet:** between the primary bedroom and the laundry block, behind the bath, entered from the bedroom through a hinged door that swings into the closet and opens flat against the hall-side wall. Hanging rods on both long sides: the one on the bath side runs to the bedroom wall; the one on the entry side stops short of it to leave room for the door and a landing inside it. No door to the bath. The far end wall is one built-in: corner shelves (shoes, folded clothes, bags) either side of a drawer stack.
- **Primary suite:** a modest south window, since it faces SSW into a sleeping room; shade it with the overhang. Optional reading nook with a built-in bench (window seat) in the primary bedroom.

- **Lobby:** exterior door at the west end of the bedroom hall, out to the west side yard. A 7' door with a fixed glass transom above it throws daylight down the long hall.

## Ceilings and roof

- **Vaulted great room:** the foyer, lounge, dining room and kitchen share one continuous sloped ceiling following the roof, highest at the clerestory and lowest at the rear wall, with no step between rooms. The foyer/TV wall runs full height up to the sloped ceiling, so the foyer is a tall space of its own, open toward the dining room. Insulation goes at the roof deck (spray foam in the rafter bays). Light fixtures, fans, smoke alarms and sprinkler heads in the vault are laid out and wired before insulation, with a few spare conduits up the rafter bays; prefer surface, pendant or slim fixtures over recessed cans. The range hood ducts straight up through the roof.
- Everywhere else: flat 9' ceilings under the sealed attic. The ducted heat pump unit, its ducts, and the ERV go in that attic (over the bedroom wing and utility block), so no hallway drop ceiling is needed.
- The clerestory glass counts toward the Title 24 glazing budget, which puts the house right at the 20% prescriptive limit: confirm with the energy consultant (a slightly shorter band, or the performance path, covers it).
- Ceiling box or low-voltage bracket at the two Wi-Fi access point locations, installed at rough-in with the Cat6A ending inside.
- Ceiling fans: short downrods under the 9' ceilings, longer ones (or sloped-ceiling mounts) in the vault.
- The whole roof faces plan-south (true azimuth ~201°, SSW, fine for solar): keep it free of vents and skylights for future solar; no tall trees south of the house.
- Solar mounts and conduit on the roof, plus a conduit run to a future battery location.
- **Class A asphalt shingle roof** over the sealed attic (unvented deck allowed by CRC R806.5). Get the roofer to confirm in writing that the shingle warranty covers an unvented deck; a dark shingle is preferred; pick a cool-rated (reflective-granule) dark shingle and confirm it meets the Title 24 roofing requirements. Roof form: a midcentury-modern **shed roof**, one slope rising from the rear wall to the street, at the minimum slope shingles allow (CRC R905.2.2): the low-slope double-underlayment method, preferably a full high-temperature peel-and-stick membrane, and a shingle line the maker warranties at that slope (in writing, along with the roofer's workmanship warranty); deep eaves and a thin dark fascia. The tall street wall carries a **clerestory band** over the lounge. No butterfly or near-flat roofs over the house. If the budget needs it, the fallback is an MCM low gable with flat ceilings throughout (see the exterior experiments).
- **Exterior walls: stucco** (noncombustible), in a light MCM palette with a dark fascia and a bright accent front door.

## Sealed attic and storage

- Insulation at the roofline: spray foam under the roof deck or rigid board above it. No attic vents.
- Attic storage loading (about 20 psf) on a strip over the bedroom wing, between the hall and the street wall, where the shed roof gives the attic its height (it tapers to very little over the rear rooms), specified to the truss designer, with plywood decking over it. Keep it clear of the equipment platform and walkway by the hatch.
- Insulated, gasketed pull-down ladder sized for 9' ceilings, in the bedroom hall across from the walk-in closet wall, where no doors are in the ladder's way. Keep the hall's Wi-Fi access point, return grille and smoke alarm clear of it. Light switch at the hatch and an outlet in the attic. Code wants at least 30" of headroom above the opening and a 30"-high, floored passage to the air handler and ERV within 20' (CRC R807.1, M1305.1.3); the builder's target is 36" at the opening, which the shed roof gives over the hall with room to spare. Put the air handler toward the street side, where the attic is tallest.
- Equipment access per mechanical code (opening, walkway, and service platform with a light and outlet) at the ducted unit and ERV. Place that equipment near the hatch.
- Thermal barrier over the spray foam (drywall or an approved coating), since the attic will be used for storage.
- Smurf tube or a cable tray across the attic from the network rack for future runs.
- Keep heavy items and heat-producing equipment such as servers out of the attic.

## Wildfire hardening

- Enclosed eaves with noncombustible or ignition-resistant soffits (wood-look is fine if it's fire-retardant-treated or metal/fiber cement).
- Ember-resistant screens or self-closing dampers on every remaining opening: ERV intake and exhaust, exhaust-fan terminations, the range-hood cap, and foundation vents if the floor is raised.
- Stucco walls (noncombustible) and tempered dual-pane windows.
- A noncombustible zone around the house (pavers/gravel, no mulch or turf), at least as wide as code requires.
- Sealed (unvented) attic with no attic vents, so embers have no entry point.
- Metal (or other noncombustible) fencing where any fence meets the house, for the code-required distance.
