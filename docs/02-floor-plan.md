# Architect: floor plan, roof, finishes

[← Back to README](../README.md)

These are the rooms, spaces, and openings the plan must provide so the trades can do their work. The plan drawing and the schematic show one way to fit them; sizes and fine details are the architect's call.

## Entry, foyer, and lounge

- **Corner porch** at the lounge/carport (northeast) corner, set into the house, with a post at the outside corner where the carport beam lands. Front step in front.
- **Front door** on the street-facing side of the porch, with a sidelight.
- **Reeded-glass window** in the porch's side wall, so you can see who's at the door from the lounge. Narrow reeds or satin-reeded, dual-pane, low-E, tempered, NFRC-rated, from the same window line as the rest of the house.
- **Foyer** with a closet and bench on the east wall. The **foyer wall** (stopping short of the lobby) separates foyer and lounge; its lounge side is the **TV wall**.
- **Lounge:** large north window centred on the wall. One L sectional, its window leg floated off the window and its other leg facing the TV wall, with a console behind that leg running the length of its seats. Square coffee table. No extra chairs (they blocked the TV sightline or the walkway). All furniture stays clear of the walkway from the foyer to the bedroom hall.

## Dining

- **Dining:** table and the south window centred on the dining room's south wall.
- **Sliding door** on the east wall, centred between the carport fence and the house's southeast corner, opening onto the dining patio.
- **South window:** large. It actually faces SSW (azimuth ~201°), so it takes strong afternoon sun: plan an overhang or exterior shade and check it against the Title 24 glazing budget.

## Kitchen

See [kitchen](08-kitchen.md). A comfortable work aisle, a 36" fridge, and a peninsula with three stools (with knee overhang) facing the dining area.

## Pantry, laundry, mechanical, powder (the block behind the kitchen)

The block is three strips, each with its own door off the main lobby (no side hall):

- **Pantry:** door at the corner by the fridge, hinged so it opens flat against the kitchen wall. Shelves on three sides (the shelves by the door start past the door leaf). Outlets inside.
- **Mechanical / network closet:** behind the pantry at the south end of the strip, opening into the laundry through a **louvered** bifold (the heat pump water heater needs the laundry's air volume). Holds the heat pump water heater, softener and brine tank, sediment pre-filter, tankless RO filter, water distribution manifold, and the network rack (kept clear of water lines). Floor drain or drain pan. No conditioned supply into the closet.
- **Laundry:** own door off the lobby, plus an alcove behind the powder room. Everything works off the south (rear) wall, which is solid (no window): open floor in front of the mech closet's bifold, then a small, shallow utility sink, then the washer and dryer side by side (not stacked) toward the primary-bath wall, with a folding counter over the machines and upper cabinets above. Leak pan under the washer. Open shelves on the alcove side of the powder-room wall; shallow full-height storage (brooms, vacuum, ironing board, supplies) on the laundry side of it. Good overhead lighting and an exhaust fan, since there is no window. Solid-core door and insulated walls to keep machine noise out of the bedroom hall.
- **Indoor electrical subpanel** flush in the laundry's pantry-side wall just past the door swing, with its code working space kept clear; nothing else on that wall.
- **Powder room:** own door off the lobby. Toilet on the south wall (shared with the laundry alcove) facing the door; small wall-hung sink by the door. Solid-core door.
- **Robot vacuum dock** niche in the laundry at the bottom of the tall storage, open to the floor; it needs water and a trapped drain (see plumbing).

## Bedroom wing

- **Gym:** North window matching the office's. Mineral wool in the walls shared with the lounge end. ERV boost control. Outlet for a stationary bike.
- **Gym ring mounts:** two ceiling points near the room's centre, swinging along the room's long direction. The truss designer must carry a concentrated load of about 500 lb at each point (blocking across several trusses with framing connectors; through-bolted forged eye bolts with plate washers). Keep lights, fans, HVAC/ERV registers, and the smoke alarm out of the swing zone. Photograph the blocking before drywall.
- **Guest:** Two closets flanking a centred door.
- **Office:** Solid-core door and insulated walls for calls; wall blocking for monitor arms and shelves; dimmable ceiling light.
- **Common bath:** a standard alcove tub with a shower head and curtain across the window end. Pick the tub model before framing so the alcove is framed to its spec. The tub's drain, valve and shower head, the WC and the vanity all share the east wall (the plumbing wall with the gym). Linen closet in the corner by the door.
- **Primary bath wet room:** a two-person jetted tub on the plumbing wall, fitting wall to wall with no end gap (pick the model first: it must have an accessible pump panel, either a freestanding tub or one serviced from the walk-in closet side; see [open questions](09-open-questions.md)), plus a curbless shower beside it with two shower heads facing each other (closet wall and rear wall), behind one fixed glass panel with a linear drain, walk in at the closet end. Bottle storage is a ledge wall: the shower's closet wall is built out below chest height into the shower, topped with a tiled shelf (sloped to drain) across the full width of the shower, with that head's valve and supply inside the thickened part. Keep the walk-in opening its full width past the ledge. Then the WC and a double vanity. Two rear windows: one centred over the tub (tempered, in the wet zone) and a small one over the WC (obscure glass). The bath's door is at the bedroom end of the walk-in closet; you reach the bath through the closet. See [plumbing](04-plumbing.md).
- **Walk-in closet:** between the primary bedroom and the laundry block, behind the bath, entered from the bedroom through a wide cased opening (no door). Hanging rods on both long sides, stopping short of the bedroom end so there's a straight path to the bath door. The far end wall is one built-in: corner shelves (shoes, folded clothes, bags) either side of a drawer stack.
- **Primary suite:** optional reading nook with a built-in bench (window seat) in the primary bedroom.

- **Lobby:** exterior door at the west end of the bedroom hall, out to the west side yard.

## Ceilings and roof

- 10' ceilings throughout. The ducted heat pump unit, its ducts, and the ERV go in the sealed attic, so no hallway drop ceiling is needed.
- Ceiling box or low-voltage bracket at the two Wi-Fi access point locations, installed at rough-in with the Cat6A ending inside.
- Ceiling fans need downrods sized for the 10' ceilings.
- Keep the plan-south roof (true azimuth ~201°, SSW, which is fine for solar) free of vents and skylights for future solar; no tall trees south of the house.
- Solar mounts and conduit on the roof, plus a conduit run to a future battery location.
- **Class A asphalt shingle roof** over the sealed attic (unvented deck allowed by CRC R806.5). Get the roofer to confirm in writing that the shingle warranty covers an unvented deck; a dark shingle is preferred; pick a cool-rated (reflective-granule) dark shingle and confirm it meets the Title 24 roofing requirements. Any roof style must stay at a slope shingles allow: no butterfly or near-flat roofs, and double underlayment on the shallower slopes. A midcentury shed or gable works at a low-but-shingle-friendly slope (see the exterior experiments).

## Sealed attic and storage

- Insulation at the roofline: spray foam under the roof deck or rigid board above it. No attic vents.
- Attic storage loading (about 20 psf) on a central strip, specified to the truss designer, with plywood decking over it.
- Insulated pull-down ladder sized for 10' ceilings, in the hallway or laundry. Light switch at the hatch and an outlet in the attic.
- Equipment access per mechanical code (opening, walkway, and service platform with a light and outlet) at the ducted unit and ERV. Place that equipment near the hatch.
- Thermal barrier over the spray foam (drywall or an approved coating), since the attic will be used for storage.
- Smurf tube or a cable tray across the attic from the network rack for future runs.
- Keep heavy items and heat-producing equipment such as servers out of the attic.

## Wildfire hardening

- Enclosed eaves with noncombustible or ignition-resistant soffits (wood-look is fine if it's fire-retardant-treated or metal/fiber cement).
- Ember-resistant screens or self-closing dampers on every remaining opening: ERV intake and exhaust, exhaust-fan terminations, the range-hood cap, and foundation vents if the floor is raised.
- Noncombustible siding and tempered dual-pane windows.
- A noncombustible zone around the house (pavers/gravel, no mulch or turf), at least as wide as code requires.
- Sealed (unvented) attic with no attic vents, so embers have no entry point.
- Metal (or other noncombustible) fencing where any fence meets the house, for the code-required distance.
