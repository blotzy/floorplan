# Preliminary zoning check

[← Back to README](../README.md)

An owner-prepared set for a first zoning read before the architect's permit drawings: is the house's size, placement and height in line with the R-1 zone and the Altadena Community Standards District (CSD)?

**The set:** [zoning-check-set.pdf](../renders/zoning/zoning-check-set.pdf) (four 11x17 sheets)

| Sheet | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| Z-1 | Site plan with setbacks (required vs provided), parking, front-yard walls, and the project data table (lot, floor area, lot coverage, CSD limit, height, bedrooms) |
| Z-2 | Floor plan with overall dimensions, room sizes and areas |
| Z-3 | Roof plan: split roof at 2:12 (shed and gable), eaves, highest point, carport roof |
| Z-4 | Four elevations with heights above finished floor |

It is a schematic, marked "not for construction", and asks for a preliminary read only. Heights are above finished floor; height above grade waits on the foundation and grading design.

## Option 1: eCheck (automated, online)

LA County's eCheck runs an automated zoning review of uploaded plans for fire rebuilds in Altadena R-1. The report can later be attached to the Disaster Recovery Permit application.

1. Go to **echeck.lacounty.gov** and create a free account.
2. Search for **220 Laurel Dr, Altadena**.
3. Upload **zoning-check-set.pdf**.
4. Choose **Non-Like-for-Like** (the layout and size differ from the house that burned).
5. Wait for the report (during the pilot, up to about 10 business days). Download it and keep it with the project files.

Check the site for any current requirements on file format or sheets before uploading; the program started as a pilot.

## Option 2: a planner (free consultation, or EPIC-LA)

**Consultation:** book a zoning consultation at the Altadena One-Stop Permit Center (in person or virtual) and bring or email the PDF. Ask for a preliminary read on the questions below.

**EPIC-LA:** the formal route is the County Disaster Recovery Permit (rebuild) application in EPIC-LA, where the zoning review is the first step. Use it once the architect is ready to submit; the zoning check set is a preview, not the permit submittal.

**Project description to paste:**

> Non-like-for-like rebuild of a single-family residence destroyed in the Eaton Fire. One story, about 2,238 sq ft (to the outside of the walls), 4 bedrooms and 3 baths, with an attached open carport (2 covered spaces, 396 sq ft) and a 37 sq ft covered porch. Lot 113.00' x 64.77' (7,319 sq ft), R-1, Altadena CSD. Lot coverage about 2,672 sq ft (36.5%). Split roof at 2:12: a shed over the great room with a clerestory over the lounge, and a low gable over the rest of the bedroom wing; top of roof about 17'-6" above finished floor at the street eave. Owner-prepared schematic for a preliminary zoning read; permit drawings to follow from the architect.

**Property details:** 220 Laurel Dr, Altadena, CA 91001 · APN 5832-003-027 · Johnston and Hay Tract, portion of Lot 11 (M.B. 9-50).

## Questions to ask

1. **Side setbacks:** is the side yard 10% of the lot width (11'-3⅝" here)? The plan provides 11'-6" each side.
2. **Rear setback:** the lot is less than 75 ft deep, so the plan uses the shallow-lot rear yard, 20% of the average depth (12'-11½"; §22.110.080.D, Rear Yards on Shallow Lots), and provides 13'-2". Confirm it applies under the CSD's rear-yard standard (25 ft for lots under 20,000 sq ft, §22.320.090.D.1.a), which supersedes only the base zone's yards (§22.18.040.C). The County's Altadena CSD summary lists the shallow-lot rule under the CSD rear yard, and the eCheck passed §22.110.080.D but separately failed the 25 ft CSD figure.
3. **Floor area and lot coverage:** do both use the CSD limit of ¼ of the net lot area plus 1,000 sq ft (about 2,830 sq ft)? Is the open carport excluded from floor area but counted in coverage? Is the net lot area the full 7,319 sq ft?
4. **Height:** what is the height limit, and from which grade is it measured on a lot that falls about 4 ft across the footprint? The shed's street eave is the high point.
5. **Carport and porch:** the plan is drawn to meet the §22.140.520 parking rules: the porch is a qualifying 8' x 5' covered entry (Table 22.140.520-D, ceiling at 9'), projecting 1' into the front yard under a canopy (§22.110.090.D.2), so the parking only has to sit behind the front door. The carport's roof is in line with the house front, but its front 4' is a covered walkway, not parking; the two spaces start at the front door's line. Please confirm that reading, and whether the street-facing garage rule for lots over 100' of frontage applies to an open carport.
6. **Parking:** are the two carport spaces enough, and can uncovered parking in the driveway count?
7. **Eaves:** 2'-6" all round, the most an eave may project into a required yard (§22.110.090.A). The front, rear and west walls sit just outside their setback lines (1¼", 2½" and 2⅜"), so the eaves reach about 2'-4¾", 2'-3½" and 2'-3⅝" into those yards. Each stays more than 2'-6" from the lot line and more than 8' above grade, with no supports in the yard. Confirm the projection is measured to the fascia.
8. **Street facade articulation:** the street windows are recessed into the wall (a Table 22.140.520-C strategy). How many strategies does the street wall need at its length (the table's count goes up with the facade's length), and do the recessed corner porch and the clerestory count toward them? If more are needed, the fallback is canopies over the street windows (allowed into the front yard under §22.110.090.B).
9. **Driveway Zone:** what are its dimensions for fences, walls and hedges near the driveway? (The entry court's seat wall is 24".)
10. **Rebuild size limit:** any limit on the rebuild's size compared with the house that burned?

Note the answers in [open questions](09-open-questions.md) and pass them to the architect.
